# FX Hedging

## Purpose

This capability focuses on understanding how companies can manage foreign exchange risk by comparing different hedging strategies. Exchange rates can change over time, which can affect how much money a company receives when doing business internationally.

## Scenario 31: Turbine-Gearbox

For this project, a U.S. wind-turbine gearbox manufacturer has invoiced a Spanish renewable-power developer for EUR 9,900,000. The company expects to receive the payment in 365 days and will need to convert the euros into U.S. dollars.

The purpose of the project is to compare different hedging strategies that can help protect the company against unfavorable exchange rate changes.

## Hedging Strategies

The project focuses on three main strategies:

- **Forward Contract:** Locks in an exchange rate to provide more certainty about the amount of U.S. dollars received.
- **Money-Market Hedge:** Uses foreign currency borrowing, currency conversion, and U.S. dollar investing to manage exchange rate risk.
- **Currency Put Option:** Provides protection against unfavorable exchange rate changes while allowing the company to benefit if the euro strengthens.

## Stage 1: Executive Brief

Stage 1 introduced the company's foreign currency exposure and explained why managing exchange rate risk is important. The executive brief discussed the potential risks and different approaches the company could consider.

## Stage 2: Model Specification

Stage 2 focuses on planning the Excel model before building it. The specification explains the required inputs, formulas, worksheet structure, assumptions, sensitivity analysis, and validation checks.

The goal is to create a clear plan that can be followed when developing and reviewing the Excel workbook in Stage 3.
