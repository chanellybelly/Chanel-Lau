# Foreign Exchange Hedging — Executive Brief

**To:** Chief Financial Officer  
**From:** Finance Team  
**Date:** October 29, 2026  
**Subject:** Managing Foreign Exchange Risk

## The Problem

The company is expecting to receive €4,500,000 from a European customer in one year. Although the amount in euros is already determined, the amount received in U.S. dollars will depend on the exchange rate at the time of payment. This creates financial risk because exchange rates are constantly changing, and a decrease in the value of the euro could negatively affect the company's cash flow.

For example, if the exchange rate is $1.10 per euro, the payment would be worth $4,950,000. However, if the euro weakens to $1.00, the company would only receive $4,500,000. This is a difference of $450,000, which could significantly affect the company's budget and financial planning. Due to this uncertainty, different hedging strategies should be considered to protect the expected payment.

## Potential Hedging Strategies

There are three main hedging strategies the company could consider.

**Forward and Futures Contracts:** These contracts allow companies to lock in an exchange rate for a future date. The main advantage is having a predictable dollar amount, which makes budgeting and financial planning easier. However, if the euro strengthens, the company would not benefit from the favorable exchange rate. Futures contracts may also involve margin requirements.

**Money-Market Hedge:** This strategy involves borrowing euros today, converting them into U.S. dollars, and investing the dollars until the payment is received. This approach provides more certainty regarding future cash flows. However, it requires borrowing and involves interest rates and additional financial transactions, making it more complicated to manage.

**Currency Options:** A currency option gives the company the right, but not the obligation, to exchange euros at a predetermined rate. This protects against a weakening euro while allowing the company to benefit if the euro strengthens. The main disadvantage is the upfront premium, which reduces the total amount received.

## Assumptions

This analysis assumes that the company will receive the full €4,500,000 in one year and that its main priority is reducing uncertainty in future cash flows. It also assumes that all three hedging strategies are available to the company. These assumptions will need to be reviewed when actual exchange rates, interest rates, and transaction costs are considered.

## Hypothesis

A forward hedge is expected to be the most suitable strategy because it allows the company to lock in an exchange rate ahead of time. While this means giving up potential gains if the euro strengthens, having predictable cash flows may be more valuable for the company's financial planning and budgeting.

## How the Hypothesis Could Be Proven Wrong

The hypothesis would be challenged if the money-market hedge provides higher guaranteed dollar proceeds than the forward contract. Additionally, if the euro strengthens enough for a currency option to generate higher net proceeds after accounting for its premium, the forward hedge may not be the most beneficial strategy under those market conditions.
